Stop — no exceptions

  • No questions about her past. Not exes, not numbers, not "just curious."
  • No checking. Phone, socials, her friends' posts, an ex's profile. Not even quickly.
  • No comments on how women look or behave. Telly, street, online. It doesn't leave my mouth.
  • No sulking. Say the true thing or say I need a bit. Never silence.
  • No fishing. No testing her, no leading questions I don't want answered.
  • No progress reports. She isn't my audience.

What to say

Short is better. Long is you asking her to make you feel better.

When you've gone cold
"I've gone a bit off in my head. It's nothing you've done. Give me half an hour."

Within two minutes of noticing. Then actually come back in half an hour and be normal. Six seconds of honesty instead of an evening of her guessing.

When she mentions an ex, a bloke, a crush

Nothing. Normal face, carry on talking. That's the whole move.

You'll want to go quiet, or make a joke with an edge on it, or ask one follow-up question. All three are the compulsion. She clocks all three.

If it's really loud, excuse yourself for five minutes rather than sit there radiating it.

When you've already snapped or sulked
"That was me, not you. Sorry. I'm working on it."

That's the whole apology. No explanation of what set you off, because the explanation is always secretly a request that she change something.

And once. Repairing three times in an evening is its own kind of pressure.

When you want reassurance

That's the one to sit on. Reassurance works for about a day and teaches your brain the feeling is an emergency someone else resolves.

If you need it said out loud, say it to a mate, not to her.

When she asks how the work is going

Short and undramatic. "Yeah, doing it. Some days are easier than others."

Don't hand her a list of what you've resisted. That makes her the examiner and puts her back in charge of your feelings.

Read when it's bad

The feeling is not information

The knot is a threat response, same machinery as a car swerving at you. It fires at a hypothetical. It feels like evidence. It isn't. Nothing has happened.

There's no fact that ends this

No answer she gives closes it, because what you actually want is to have been her only person ever, and that isn't available. Chasing it just trains the chase.

It isn't love, it's fear

Love wants her to have a big life. Fear wants her to have a small one. When you're upset she found someone attractive, nothing generous is happening. That's a man scanning for threats.

The answer isn't less love. It's less fear.

The gripping is what loses people

Not one big incident. The accumulated weight of being someone's security blanket, having to think about how every small thing will land.

Coldness reads as punishment

From inside it's armour. From where she's standing it looks like she's done something and you're withholding. She'll spend the evening working out what she did wrong on a day she did nothing.

You're not going to feel fixed

There's no unlock. One day you'll notice you didn't ask the question, and it won't have been hard. That's what better looks like. Undramatic. You'll almost miss it.

Doing it only for her is the version that fails

If the only fuel is fear of losing her, you stop the day she seems safe again. This has to be who you want to be either way, or it doesn't hold.

Every week

  • One thing that isn't her. A mate, the gym, football. A man with his own life is harder to frighten.
  • Twenty minutes reading. Same time each week, so it doesn't need motivation.
  • Tell one person something true about how you're actually doing.
